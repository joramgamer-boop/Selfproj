# Starting point: Mills is the first sociology text

Joram is reading Mills' "The Promise" as assigned coursework and stated this is
their first sociology text — no prior vocabulary can be assumed, including terms
Mills himself treats as ordinary (_milieu_, _social structure_, _biography_ as a
technical term). They want four outcomes at once: follow the argument, analyse
real cases, explain it aloud, and write about it.

**Implications.** The four outcomes are not equally reachable from zero, so the
ordering chosen is *analyse real cases first*, via the troubles/issues
distinction. Reasons: it is the tool Mills himself calls "the most fruitful
distinction" (p. 8); it is taught through worked examples rather than
definitions, which suits a first-timer; and competence with it is what the other
three outcomes are built on — you cannot explain the sociological imagination
aloud, or write about it, without being able to do the split.

Not yet known: whether Joram can hold Mills' *prose* (as distinct from his
ideas). Chapter 1 is rhetorically dense 1959 writing. Watch for this in the next
session — if decoding sentences is the bottleneck rather than the concepts, the
next lesson should be a close-reading exercise on a single paragraph rather than
more conceptual ground.
